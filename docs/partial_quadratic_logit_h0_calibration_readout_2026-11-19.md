The exploratory signal is asymmetric: gamma=-1 shifts the median bias downward relative to gamma=0, while gamma=+1 shifts it upward. The paired contrasts are more informative than comparing unpaired cell means, but seven replicates remain inadequate for a confirmatory claim.

### Validation-size contrast

From n=100 to n=500, mean signed bias changed by:
- gamma=-1: -1.04 [-1.54, -0.53];
- gamma=0: -1.57 [-2.75, -0.39];
- gamma=+1: -0.79 [-1.91, +0.32].

These are paired t intervals over the same seven replicates. The results suggest only modest movement in median bias as validation size increases; the gamma=+1 contrast interval includes zero. The central 68% interval widths also remain broadly similar. This is not evidence that larger validation samples are useless: the experiment is tiny, conditions on four detected events, and the posterior is also affected by event noise, catalogue sampling, model misspecification, and the fixed H0 grid.

## Interpretation and limits

The most notable preliminary pattern is that the degree-one fitted inclusion model has a larger positive H0 median bias at gamma=+1 than at gamma=0, with a paired difference around +6 to +7 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹ in these seven replicates. The gamma=-1 contrast is negative. This is consistent with a curvature-direction sensitivity in this synthetic setup, but it is not yet a stable calibration result.

Several caveats are decisive:

- Seven replicates per cell yield very wide coverage uncertainty. For example, 4/7 coverage has a Wilson 95% interval of approximately [0.25, 0.84].
- The experiment fixes the number of detected GW events at four; it does not test event-count/rate calibration.
- The 141-point H0 grid has spacing 2 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹. The separate grid-refinement audit must establish numerical stability before the posterior summaries are treated as grid-converged.
- Coefficient-quadrature convergence is not H0-grid convergence.
- The pilot is a low-redshift synthetic Euclidean benchmark, not LVK/GWTC or real galaxy survey analysis.
- No joint simulation-based calibration (SBC), PIT calibration, or confirmatory frequentist coverage conclusion is established here.
