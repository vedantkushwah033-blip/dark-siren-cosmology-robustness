# Known-host calibration surface protocol

**Status:** implementation, focused tests, and a manually dispatchable workflow are committed. Numerical findings are pending a successful run and independent raw-artifact audit.  
**Scope:** a synthetic one-host calibration diagnostic, not a real gravitational-wave analysis or an observational estimate of H0.

## Research question

When candidate-host mixture uncertainty is removed by supplying the true host redshift, how do fixed-truth coverage, error, and posterior width vary with the true H0 and assumed fractional distance uncertainty? The experiment also checks whether prior-boundary effects remain visible in this deliberately simplified setting.

This experiment isolates one source of uncertainty; it does not by itself validate the simulator, detector selection function, galaxy catalogues, or astrophysical realism.

## Design

- True H0: 50, 55, 60, 65, 70, 75, 80, 85, 90 km s^-1 Mpc^-1.
- Fractional distance-noise levels: 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20.
- Default replication: 300 records per truth/noise cell, giving 36 cells and 10,800 raw records.
- Prior: uniform H0 support [50, 90] km s^-1 Mpc^-1.
- Posterior grid: 1,601 equally spaced points on [50, 90].
- Data generation: one host drawn from the synthetic galaxy catalogue using luminosity weighting; distance generated by the pinned original simulator at the selected true H0.
- Inference: the known host redshift is supplied to a single-host likelihood. Generation and inference use the same low-redshift distance approximation in this experiment.
- Pairing: within a fixed truth and replicate, the same catalogue seed, host-selection seed, and distance-noise seed are used at all four fractional noise levels. The simulator commit is pinned in the workflow and recorded in metadata.

Truths H0 = 50 and 90 lie exactly on the prior boundaries. They are intentional boundary stress tests and must not be pooled uncritically with interior truths when discussing calibration.

## Pre-specified outcomes

For every truth/noise cell, report:

1. Empirical coverage of nominal 68% and 95% credible intervals, including success counts, Wilson score intervals, and binomial Monte Carlo standard errors.
2. Median bias, mean posterior-median error, mean posterior-mean error, and RMSE for both posterior summaries.
3. Mean 68% and 95% interval widths and mean posterior standard deviation.
4. Mean and standard deviation of posterior-median pulls.
5. Fraction of posterior medians within 0.5 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 of either prior edge, and an explicit boundary-truth flag.

The 36 cell summaries are descriptive. The primary comparisons should first be plotted and tabulated by true H0, then by noise level; do not present an overall average as if it established uniform calibration. Paired differences across noise levels may be estimated from replicate-matched raw records. If bootstrap intervals are added, the bootstrap unit must be the replicate, not the individual posterior-grid point.

## Validation and audit requirements

Before interpreting results:

- Run the focused tests and the complete Python 3.10/3.11/3.12 test matrix.
- Confirm exactly 9 × 4 × replicates raw records and 36 summary cells.
- Confirm all numeric fields are finite; posterior summaries and interval endpoints remain within prior support; standard deviations and interval widths are positive; 68% intervals are nested within 95% intervals.
- Recompute coverage, Wilson intervals, errors, RMSE, interval widths, and edge fractions from the raw records in an independent audit script or notebook. Compare with the stored summaries.
- Check that all 36 cells have the expected replicate count and no duplicate (truth, noise, replicate) keys.
- Verify seed pairing by comparing catalogue/host information and standardized noise across the four noise levels within each truth/replicate.
- Record artifact name, workflow URL, source commits, artifact SHA-256, dependency versions, and any excluded or failed runs.

The workflow's embedded checks are a first-pass guardrail, not a substitute for independent post-download recomputation.

## Interpretation limits

- This is a synthetic single-host experiment with a matched, low-redshift distance model. It does not use real LVK strain data, a real detection pipeline, or a validated survey catalogue.
- The known-host assumption removes candidate-host ambiguity by design; it should not be described as a realistic representation of every dark-siren event.
- Fixed-truth frequentist coverage and prior-predictive simulation-based calibration answer different questions. Success in one does not imply success in the other.
- Boundary truths are expected to behave differently from interior truths under a bounded prior. Report them separately and avoid calling boundary undercoverage a universal property of the method.
- A narrow posterior is not evidence of accurate or calibrated inference. Interpret width alongside bias, RMSE, and coverage.
- A passing CI suite validates only the software properties actually tested. It does not establish scientific realism or support observational claims.
