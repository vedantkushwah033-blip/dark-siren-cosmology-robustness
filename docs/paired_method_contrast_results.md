# Paired method contrasts: bootstrap results

## Provenance

This report summarizes paired contrasts calculated from the `per_run.csv` artifact produced by the successful coverage-transition workflow. Each row represents one generated detected observation reused for corrected and uncorrected inference. The comparison is paired within each truth/prior cell.

- Successful experiment: https://github.com/vedantkushwah033-blip/dark-siren-cosmology-robustness/actions/runs/40075800237
- The run completed successfully on Python 3.10, 3.11, and 3.12.
- Per-run artifact: 5,400 rows (9 truths × 2 priors × 300 replicates).
- Contrasts: 126 rows (9 truths × 2 priors × 7 metrics).
- Bootstrap: 5,000 paired-observation resamples per cell/metric, seed 20261026; percentile 95% intervals.

The contrast CSV is generated by `scripts/run_coverage_transition_map.py` as `paired_method_contrasts.csv`. Results below were independently recomputed from the downloaded per-run CSV using the same statistic and bootstrap seed. The CSV artifacts should be retained with the workflow run for auditability.

## Main findings

The contrast is corrected minus uncorrected. For absolute error and squared error, negative values favor the corrected method. For coverage indicators, positive values mean corrected inference covered the truth more frequently. For interval width, negative values mean narrower corrected intervals.

The paired coverage comparison makes the tradeoff explicit: correction generally improves 68% coverage at lower truths but reduces it at high truths, particularly at 85 and 88. The zero contrast at truth 52 under [50,90] reflects both methods missing the truth in all 300 replications; it is not evidence of good calibration.

### Interval width

Correction tends to narrow 68% intervals at lower truths, with the largest mean differences here near truths 52–60 (about −0.56 to −2.16 units, depending on prior). At truths 70–88 the direction varies: under [50,90], corrected intervals are wider on average for truths 70, 75, 80, 85, and 88. Narrower intervals are not inherently better if coverage deteriorates.

### Squared error

The paired squared-error contrast agrees with the absolute-error pattern: correction reduces squared error at truths 52–65; it increases squared error at truths 75–88. For truth 70 under [50,90], the interval includes zero, while under [40,100] the corrected-minus-uncorrected mean squared-error difference is positive and its bootstrap interval excludes zero.

## Interpretation

1. **The selection correction is not uniformly superior.** Its apparent advantage changes across the truth range and prior support.
2. **Prior support modifies the method comparison.** The contrast at truth 70 changes direction for absolute error between [50,90] and [40,100].
3. **Coverage, point-estimation error, and interval width can disagree.** They should be reported together.
4. **Paired resampling respects the design.** Each bootstrap resample samples the per-replicate corrected-minus-uncorrected difference, not two unrelated samples.
5. **Bootstrap uncertainty is conditional.** It reflects Monte Carlo variation under the specified toy generator and does not account for uncertainty about the physical realism of the model.

## Limitations

This is a simplified synthetic low-redshift host-mixture model, not a real LVK analysis or a real galaxy-catalogue inference. Only nine truths, two prior supports, and 300 replications per truth were studied. Bootstrap intervals are percentile intervals and may be coarse for binary coverage outcomes. No claim is made that these findings generalize to real events or all selection functions.
